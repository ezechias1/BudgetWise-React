## What BudgetWise Junior is

**A kids' budgeting feature built into BudgetWise.** It lives inside Family mode — no separate app, no separate login for the parent, no extra subscription. Once it launches, any parent already paying R79/month for BudgetWise Pro gets the whole thing included.

It's designed for children **ages 10 to 13** — old enough to run the app on their own, young enough that teaching still feels valuable to the parent.

It uses **virtual money with a real-world settle-up**. The app does **not** move money, does **not** need a bank card, and does **not** partner with any bank. Your child's "balance" is a record of what you, the parent, owe them for the chores and lessons they've completed. When you pay them in cash or by EFT, you tap "Mark as paid" and the ledger updates. Simple, transparent, and fully under your control.

## How it helps kids learn about budgeting

Financial literacy research keeps saying the same thing: kids learn money by **doing money**, not by being told about money. BudgetWise Junior is built around that.

**1. Earning connects effort to reward.**
Your child sees "I did the dishes → parents owe me R10." That tiny loop — *action leads to money* — is the foundation of every financial decision they'll make as an adult.

**2. Jars make saving automatic instead of optional.**
Your child picks how to split their earnings — say 50% Save, 30% Spend, 20% Give. Every time you settle up, the split happens automatically. They don't have to remember to "put money away" — the app builds the habit for them.

**3. Goals make waiting feel worth it.**
Your child picks one real goal (a skateboard, a concert ticket, a new phone). Every rand they save gets them closer. The app shows the progress bar growing. That's the direct, visceral experience of delayed gratification — the single most predictive money skill in adult life.

**4. Daily lessons turn budgeting into something they actually enjoy.**
Short, 2-to-3-minute lessons pop up each day. They teach things like: *needs vs wants*, *how a bank works*, *why compound interest matters*, *what "needs" really means*. The app pulls their own numbers into the lessons — *"Last week you earned R45. If you'd saved 50% instead of 20%, your goal would be R11 closer."* Personalised, never abstract.

**5. Allowance isn't pretend, and isn't forgotten.**
The biggest failure mode of chore charts is: the kid does the chore, the parent forgets to pay, the kid stops trusting the system. BudgetWise Junior fixes this by showing the parent "You owe Sarah R85" every Sunday until they settle up. The promise gets kept.

## The parent's role

You are in control of everything. **The app never credits, charges, or pays without your approval.**

Specifically:

| Decision | Who chooses |
|---|---|
| Whether to add the child | You |
| The child's 4-digit PIN | You |
| What chores exist | You |
| How much each chore is worth (in rand) | You |
| Whether a completed chore counts | You (you approve or reject) |
| Which lessons are worth money | You |
| How much each lesson is worth | You |
| When to pay | You |
| How to pay (cash / EFT / other) | You |

Your child can:
- See the chores you've assigned them
- Mark them done (the app then waits for your approval)
- Do their daily money lesson
- Pick their own Save / Spend / Give jar split
- Pick a savings goal
- See what's owed to them

Your child **cannot**:
- Create chores
- Change the reward amounts
- See your personal finances, bank accounts, or other children's data
- Spend or send money (there is no card and no payments feature)
- Edit anything once the parent has approved it

### What you'll actually do each week

- **Day-to-day:** You'll get a small notification when your child marks a chore as done. You tap to approve or reject — two seconds.
- **Sunday evening:** You get one summary notification: *"This week you owe Sarah R85 and Tom R40. Tap to settle up."* You settle in cash or EFT in real life, then tap "Mark as paid" in the app.
- **Occasionally:** You set a reward amount on a new lesson when you want your child to earn money for learning. You can also add a new chore or remove an old one.

That's the whole commitment. Under five minutes a week for meaningful, ongoing financial education.

## Why this model, and not a debit card

Apps like Greenlight and GoHenry give kids a real debit card. They're excellent products — and they charge $5–$10 per child per month because the bank has to pay for cards, compliance, and transactions.

BudgetWise Junior is different on purpose:

- **No card means no fee.** Included free in BudgetWise Pro.
- **No real money through the app** means no regulatory overhead, no banking partnerships, no failed transfers.
- **Parent stays the source of the money** — your child asks you, you settle up. That's the exact habit they'll need as an adult negotiating wages, invoices, and rent.
- **Works for any family with any bank** — or no bank at all. Works the same for cash households as for digitally-native ones.

Kids learning the *habits* of saving, goal-setting, and delayed gratification is a bigger predictor of adult financial health than whether they had a card at age 12. That's the evidence we're designing against.
